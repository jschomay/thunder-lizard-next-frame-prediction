Drawing the frame in passes (diffusion, H16)

Why the terrain smears, how masked diffusion tries to fix it, and how it plugs into the U-Net we already have.

1. Why guessing every cell at once smears

Today the net writes all 2025 cells in one go, and each cell is decided alone: it can't see what its neighbours picked. Suppose a strip of new terrain is 50/50 grass or dirt, and in reality it's a few clean blobs of each. Toy example, not the net:

diffusion pass 0 / 4
  • Argmax (what we use): every cell picks its slight favourite, so the whole strip becomes one type. Repeat for 100 frames and the most common terrain spreads: smear.
  • Sampling every cell (H5, refuted): every cell rolls its own die, which gives speckle.
  • Diffusion: fix a few cells, then guess again seeing them, so the next cells can join a blob instead of guessing alone.

3. Where the U-Net fits

The U-Net is the network (the shape of the layers). Diffusion is the recipe for training it and calling it. Stable Diffusion uses a U-Net too. We keep ours and add one input, the draft, through a small new layer that starts at zero, so at the start the model behaves exactly like best-so-far.

Only terrain is diffused. Dinos and the player still come from the fate head with dice, exactly as now, so the test is about terrain alone.

7. The test

  • H16: warm start from best-so-far, 3 epochs (~20–25 min on the Mac, so no Colab needed). Compare to s3-unet-more2, which got the same 3 extra epochs without diffusion. Watch max_share (~68 → real 59), static, continuity, dino numbers unchanged.
  • H17: score the same model at 1, 2, 4 and 8 passes, and time it. 4 passes ≈ 64 ms in the browser against a 70 ms tick.
  • If max_share doesn't move after a solid run: H16 🔴, on to the transformer.
Result (2026-10-21): H16 🔴. More passes → fewer stray cells (0.24% → 0.08%; real 0.02%) but more smear (biggest terrain 69% → 73%; one-shot 68%, real 59%). Sampling instead of argmax: 77%. The passes make neighbours agree, but on the majority. Smear builds up over many frames of the net reading its own output, which a per-frame sampler doesn't touch. Full table in the journal.
