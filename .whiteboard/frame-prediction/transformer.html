A transformer that remembers (H18)

How attention works, why it might fix continuity (70 vs 95 in the real game), how it plugs into our CNN, and where it runs.

1. Tokens and attention, on a real game

A transformer works on a list of tokens. Ours: each frame cut into 9×9 patches (25 per frame), each tagged with its screen position and its age. In every layer, each token asks "which other tokens matter to me?", scores all of them, and takes a weighted mix of the best. The scoring is learned.

Below is a real backtracking game: the player walked away and came back. Click a patch in "now", then pick an old frame from the history strip. The old frame is shaded by how much each patch looks like the one you clicked. That's the job attention has to learn: find the old patch showing the same place, even though the view has moved. (The shading is a simple terrain-mix comparison standing in for the trained net.)

2. How it differs from our CNN

CNN / U-Net (ours)Transformer
What a cell sees per layerIts 3×3 neighboursAny token, anywhere
Far awayNeeds many layers (the U-Net shrinks the grid)One step
TimeA fixed window of 4 framesA sequence; can look at any old frame
Built-in assumptions"Nearby matters", "same rule everywhere"Almost none, so it needs more data
CostGrows with cellsGrows with tokens squared

Why it might help: to redraw a place you come back to, the net needs a frame from 20–120 ticks ago. The CNN never sees it; the GRU (H15) had to keep it in memory and didn't. Here the old frames are simply in the input.
Why it might not: most cells can be predicted from the last frame, so the net can ignore the old ones and still score well. That's H15's lesson, and why H19 adds extra loss weight on the row or column a scroll reveals, the only cells where memory helps.

3. Our hybrid: the CNN stays, a memory branch is added

Because the branch starts at zero, training begins exactly at our best model and only adds what memory contributes. This shape (a CNN for local detail, a transformer across time) is a common pattern in world models such as Genie.

5. Terms you'll hear

  • World model: predicts how a world changes given actions. Our CNN already is one (like GameNGen and Genie); the transformer may make it a better one.
  • Encoder-decoder: one stack reads the input, a second writes the output (the original 2017 transformer, for translation). Decoder-only: one stack, one sequence, predict what comes next (GPT). Ours is closest to an encoder with "next frame" query tokens: it reads the whole history at once and writes one frame.
  • Causal masking: tokens can't look at the future. Not needed here, since the history is all past.
