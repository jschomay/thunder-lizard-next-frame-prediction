| H1 | Predicting a semantic state grid (dino = body cell + direction + level), then rendering, beats predicting rendered frames, for the same model size. | Train approach A on both encodings. Compare changed-cell accuracy and rollout quality. | 🟡 |
| H2 | CNN (A) beats transformer (B) on small data. B wins on dino behavior at scale. LLM (C) is worst and slowest. | Same dataset and metrics across A/B/C. Vary dataset size. | 🟡 |
| H3 | History is needed: k=1 can't infer the hidden movement phase (the player moves every 2nd tick with buffered input), so it plateaus. k≥2 nearly solves stage 1. Dinos need 2–4 frames for momentum. | Sweep k ∈ {1,2,3,4}. Compare changed-cell accuracy on stage 1 and dino-cell accuracy on stage 3. *(Revised 2026-10-05 after the harness finding.)* Early hint: a hand-written 2-frame rule beats the 1-frame rule on stage 1, 68.6% vs 40.1% dynamic accuracy. **Result 2026-10-05: k=1 peaked at 22.6% dynamic acc; k=2 reached 65.5% and was still rising.** **k=4 (3 epochs): 71.6%, beats k=2 (65.9%) and the hand rule (68.6%); water errors −40%.** | 🟢 supported (k=1 vs 2) |
| H4 | Warm-starting each stage from the previous stage's weights trains faster than starting from scratch. | Stage N from scratch vs. from the stage N-1 checkpoint. Compare loss curves. **2026-10-06 (stage 1→2):** warm 70.7% at 0.25 epoch vs fresh 39.0%; final 73.7% vs 72.5%. | 🟢 supported |

| H7 | Per-step accuracy doesn't predict human-judged "feel." | H10 | A "copy by default" model (predicts changes relative to the last frame) learns faster and gets far higher exact-frame accuracy than one that redraws every cell. | Residual/copy-prior head vs plain head, same budget. | 🟢 supported: stage 1, k=4, 1 epoch from scratch: copy prior ahead throughout (dynamic 42% vs 33% at 0.25 ep; 64.7% vs 62.6% at 1 ep) and exact frames 47% vs 10% (2026-10-09) |
| H11 | Splitting the entity output into *what's here* (empty/player/dino/skeleton) and *its facing/level* stops the player vanishing in argmax rollouts. | Rollout player-survival rate, split head vs flat 42-class head. | 🟢 argmax rollouts: player present in 100% of frames (was vanishing in 4/6); sampling still multiplies |
| H12 | The model learns calibrated lava-spread odds (≈ the measured per-terrain table); sampled rollouts reproduce real lava growth curves while argmax rollouts freeze lava. | Predicted vs empirical spread probability per terrain; lava-area-over-time in rollouts (sample vs argmax vs real). **2026-10-06:** calibration off: spread over-predicted 2–17× (DIRT ≈ right). **Rollouts:** argmax freezes lava ✓; sampled lava grows at ~50–78% of the real rate, because frontier lava burns out too early (4.4%/tick vs ~0%). | 🟡 closed, partly supported: per-step odds miscalibrated (spread 2–17× over), but hybrid lava dice plays well (Jeff: "lava works quite well") |
| H13 | Predicting a *fate* per existing entity (stay/move up/right/down/left/die/gone) and moving it ourselves, plus sampling new dinos only at the screen edge, keeps the number of dinos realistic and their identity consistent in rollouts, unlike per-cell occupancy. | Rollout dino count vs real, and frames until dino count drifts; one-step fate accuracy on dinos that moved. | 🟢 supported: rollout dinos 1.50/frame vs ~1.5 real with revealed-edge spawns (per-cell argmax: 8.7, max 182); no duplication/vanishing (s3-unet-more, 2026-10-09) |
| H14 | Shifting already-visible terrain by the scroll (taken from the player's predicted move) and letting the net draw only newly revealed cells and lava changes removes smearing and the scroll/dino/glyph pinning, making play feel like a game. | Play-test (H7); rollout plausibility stats: terrain class proportions and blob structure vs real, ticks where the player moves but nothing scrolls. | ⛔ rejected: moves too much of the game into code (Jeff, 2026-10-09); built, then reverted |
